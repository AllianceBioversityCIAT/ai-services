Your assessment must be strictly aligned with CGIAR definitions and should not rely on generic or external environmental interpretations beyond CGIAR’s framework.

**CGIAR Key Objectives**:

CGIAR has set TWO specific objectives and/or collective global targets to address Environmental Health and Biodiversity in developing countries by 2030 as follows:

Stay within planetary and regional environmental boundaries: consumptive water uses in food production of less than 2500 km3 per year (with a focus on the most stressed basins), zero net deforestation, nitrogen application of 90 Tg per year (with a redistribution towards low-input farming systems) and increased use efficiency; and phosphorus application of 10 Tg per year

Maintain the genetic diversity of seed varieties, cultivated plants and farmed and domesticated animals and their related wild species, including through soundly managed gene banks at the national, regional, and international levels.

These targets provide contextual guidance only and should not be used in isolation to justify a score.

**Important Scope Clarification**:
The objectives for Climate Adaptation and Mitigation should NOT be considered when assigning the Environmental Health and Biodiversity score. Climate-related outcomes (e.g., carbon sequestration or resilience) should only be considered here if they are clearly linked to environmental integrity or biodiversity conservation, not climate action alone.

**Evaluation Criteria**:

Assign the impact area tag to the research result based on the following criteria:

NOT TARGETED (0): The research result has been screened against the CGIAR key objectives for Environmental Health and Biodiversity as highlighted in the ** CGIAR Key Objectives **, and it is NOT found to address or directly contribute to any aspect of Environmental Health and Biodiversity significantly as highlighted in the ** CGIAR Key Objectives ** i.e., the research is unrelated to the areas of CGIAR key objectives of Environmental Health and Biodiversity, focusing instead on different topics with “non-significant” or “indirect” implications for the ** CGIAR Key Objectives **.

SIGNIFICANT (1): The research result makes a direct and meaningful contribution to one or more aspects of the CGIAR key objectives for Environmental Health and Biodiversity as highlighted in the **CGIAR Key Objectives**, however contributing to the **CGIAR Key Objectives** is NOT the principal objective of the research. Environmental Health and Biodiversity as highlighted in the **CGIAR Key Objectives** are important and deliberate/explicit objectives of the research but these are NOT the primary focus of the research and only secondary to other principal goals of the research i.e., the research has other prime objectives but has been formulated or adjusted to contribute to one or more aspects of the CGIAR key objectives for Environmental Health and Biodiversity as highlighted in the **CGIAR Key Objectives**

PRINCIPAL (2): The primary/principal objective of the research result is to address and directly contribute to one or more aspects of the CGIAR key objectives for Environmental Health and Biodiversity as highlighted in the **CGIAR Key Objectives** i.e., these objectives are fundamental to the design of the research and its expected results. The project is designed to primarily achieve one or more aspects of the CGIAR key objectives for Environmental Health and Biodiversity as highlighted in the **CGIAR Key Objectives** and the research would not have been undertaken without the intention to contribute to these **CGIAR Key Objectives**.

Important QA Guidance: Scores of PRINCIPAL (2) should be used sparingly. Across all five Impact Areas, it is expected that no more than two Impact Areas receive a score of 2 for a single result, except in rare cases.
