**Definition: Innovation Use Level**

The CGIAR IPSR Innovation Use Levels framework is strictly structured around two core dimensions for assessing the current extent of innovation use:
Distance from the origin (project lead organization / developers) — This is measured by user type hierarchy. Higher levels mean use has spread farther outward from the original project:
Level 0: No use at all.
Level 1: Project Lead Organization (internal use only by the leading developer(s))
Levels 2–3: Partners (organizations directly involved in the initial innovation development)
Levels 4–5: Connected Next-Users (organizations connected/linked to initial partners but NOT directly involved in development)
Levels 6–7: Unconnected Next-Users (independent organizations with NO connection to initial development or partners)
Levels 8–9: End-Users / Beneficiaries (ultimate direct users, e.g., farmers, households, communities — not involved in development at all)

The level is determined by the farthest user type that has documented use (even if rare there). If use reaches end-users, score 8 or 9 regardless of lower-level use

Proportion/intensity of use within that user category — Indicated by (c) Common vs (R) Rare:
Common (c): Substantial, widespread, or majority proportion within that user group (e.g., most partners, a large share of connected next-users).
Rare (R): Limited, minority, sporadic, or isolated proportion (e.g., only a few partners out of many, occasional cases).

This is proportion-based (relative to the size of the group/category), NOT absolute numbers. Example: If 3 out of 5 partners use it → likely common; 2 out of 5 → rare. Use contextual judgment but require evidence to justify.

**Your Task**

Using the provided evidence text, you must:

Identify explicit statements of use of the innovation.

Determine who is using it, where, and under what conditions.

Assign the single highest Innovation Use Level (0–9) that is clearly and explicitly supported by the evidence.

If no use is explicitly documented, assign 0 (No use).

**CGIAR IPSR Guidance for Predicting Innovation Use Level**
Assessment process — follow exactly these 3 steps every time, in order:

STEP 1: Carefully read and match the provided description/evidence to the exact level descriptions from the infographic (user types 0–9).

STEP 2: Identify the highest (farthest) user type reached. Then evaluate the proportion within that category to decide (c) or (R). If evidence shows use at multiple levels, the score is based on the highest level achieved.

STEP 3: Demand and scrutinize evidence strictly for BOTH dimensions:
  - Acceptable evidence: Specific, verifiable documentation naming user types (e.g., partner lists, adoption surveys, monitoring reports, publications, validated case studies) AND indicating proportion/extent (e.g., "adopted by 7 out of 10 partners", "used by most connected networks", "only 2 independent organizations reported use").
  - Weak/inadequate evidence: Anecdotal claims, vague statements ("some users", "being used"), self-reported without specifics, or no mention of proportion/user types → downgrade the level or reject the claim (default to lower level or 0).
  - No evidence at all → assign Level 0.

Be objective, conservative, and rigorous. Never inflate levels. If evidence is ambiguous, choose the lower plausible level.

**Critical Decision Rules (Very Important)**

Evidence must explicitly mention use, not just:

dissemination,

training,

awareness,

demonstrations,

piloting without uptake,

intent or plans to use.

Mentions of testing, pilots, or trials do not qualify for levels 8 or 9.

Levels 8 and 9 require autonomous, real-world use beyond project control.

Level 9 requires evidence of performance or benefit, not just adoption.

If evidence shows different use levels across sites or countries, assign the highest level only if explicitly supported.

When evidence is unclear, select the more conservative (lower) level.

Do not introduce claims which are not present in the text.

Do not assume missing information.
