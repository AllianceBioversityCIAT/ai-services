Your goal is to accurately assign ONE of the THREE following impact area tags that reflects the relevance and impact of the research results on these CGIAR key objectives:

Your assessment must be strictly aligned with CGIAR definitions and should not rely on generic or external climate interpretations beyond what is relevant to CGIAR’s framework.

**CGIAR Key Objectives**:

CGIAR has set THREE specific objectives and/or collective global targets to address Climate Mitigation and Adaptation in developing countries as follows:

Turn agriculture and forest systems into a net sink for carbon by 2050

Equip 500 million small-scale producers to be more resilient by 2030

Support countries in implementing National Adaptation Plans and Nationally Determined Contributions, and increased ambition in climate actions by 2030 with the help of education or training

These targets provide context but should not be used alone to justify a score.

Please note that the objectives for Environmental Health and Biodiversity should not be considered as part of the objectives for Climate Mitigation and Adaptation. The objectives for Environmental Health and Biodiversity are given below, and these should NOT be considered while assigning the Impact Area tag for Climate Mitigation and Adaptation:

Stay within planetary and regional environmental boundaries: consumptive water uses in food production of less than 2500 km3 per year (with a focus on the most stressed basins), zero net deforestation, nitrogen application of 90 Tg per year (with a redistribution towards low-input farming systems) and increased use efficiency; and phosphorus application of 10 Tg per year

Maintain the genetic diversity of seed varieties, cultivated plants and farmed and domesticated animals and their related wild species, including through soundly managed gene banks at the national, regional, and international levels.

**Operational definitions (CGIAR)**:
Adaptation (CGIAR-relevant): Evidence in the text that the result aims to reduce climate risk or strengthen resilience to climate hazards/variability (e.g., drought, floods, heat), by improving capacity to anticipate, absorb, recover, or adjust.
Mitigation (CGIAR-relevant): Evidence in the text that the result aims to reduce greenhouse gas emissions or increase carbon sequestration/carbon sinks in agriculture/forestry/land use.
Policy support (NAP/NDC): Evidence the result explicitly supports NAP/NDC design, implementation, MRV, climate policy ambition, or training/education for climate action.
Direct & intentional contribution: The text explicitly states climate adaptation/mitigation as an objective, design driver, intended outcome, or core use-case—not merely a side benefit or vague framing.

**Examples of explicit signals that may indicate climate relevance (Non-exhaustive)**:
“mentions work on climate hazards/variability and resilience/risk reduction outcomes
“mentions GHG emissions, carbon footprint, sequestration, net sink, low-emission practices”
“mentions NAP/NDC support, MRV, climate policy implementation, climate-action training”
“mentions climate services (forecasting, advisories) explicitly tied to adaptation decisions”
“mentions development of climate-resilient varieties/breeds explicitly for climate stressors”
These examples are illustrative only and must be interpreted strictly within CGIAR’s definitions of Climate Adaptation and Mitigation.

**Evaluation Criteria**
Assign the impact area tag based on the direct and intentional relevance of the research result to Climate Adaptation and Mitigation as defined above.

NOT TARGETED (0): The research result has been screened against the CGIAR key objectives for Climate Adaptation and Mitigation as highlighted in the **CGIAR Key Objectives**, and it is NOT found to address or directly contribute to any aspect of Climate Adaptation and Mitigation significantly as highlighted in the **CGIAR Key Objectives** i.e., the research is unrelated to the areas of CGIAR key objectives of Climate Adaptation and Mitigation, focusing instead on different topics (i.e., productivity) with “non-significant” or “indirect” implications for the **CGIAR Key Objectives**. This includes results with only inferred, or incidental climate relevance, generic language such as “climate-smart” without concrete adaptation or mitigation-relevant results;

SIGNIFICANT (1): The research result makes a direct and meaningful contribution to one or more aspects of the CGIAR key objectives for Climate Adaptation and Mitigation as highlighted in the **CGIAR Key Objectives**, however contributing to the **CGIAR Key Objectives** is NOT the principal objective of the research. Climate Adaptation and Mitigation as highlighted in one or more aspects of the **CGIAR Key Objectives** are important and deliberate/explicit objectives of the research but these are NOT the primary focus of the research and only secondary to other principal goals of the research i.e., the research has other prime objectives but has been formulated or adjusted to contribute to one or more aspects of the CGIAR key objectives for Climate Adaptation and Mitigation as highlighted in the **CGIAR Key Objectives**. Climate-related objectives are explicit and deliberate but secondary to other main goals.

PRINCIPAL (2): The primary/principal objective of the research result is to address and directly contribute to one or more aspects of the CGIAR key objectives for Climate Adaptation and Mitigation as highlighted in the **CGIAR Key Objectives** i.e., these objectives are fundamental to the design of the research and its expected results. The project is designed to primarily achieve one or more aspects of the CGIAR key objectives for Climate Adaptation and Mitigation as highlighted in the **CGIAR Key Objectives** and the research would not have been undertaken without the intention to contribute to these **CGIAR Key Objectives**.

**Important QA Guidance**:

Scores of PRINCIPAL (2) should be used sparingly. Across all five Impact Areas, it is expected that no more than two Impact Areas receive a score of 2 for a single result, except in very rare cases.
